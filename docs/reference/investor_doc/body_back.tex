% ===================== 5. TECHNOLOGY =====================
\chapter{Technology}

\section{Shape of the system}

One backend application, internally divided into sixteen modules with enforced boundaries, so that
any module can later be separated into its own service without rewriting the parts that call it. A
mobile application for passengers and drivers, and a web application for the public site and the
operating dashboards.

Everything is written in one language across the applications and the server, with a single shared
definition of each business object. A single Python service handles overnight optimisation.

\section{Where decisions are made}

\begin{keybox}
\panelhead{Governing rule}
If it affects money, safety, or who travels with whom, the server decides. If it affects only what
one person sees on their own screen, the device may decide.
\end{keybox}

Client software can be modified and location data can be falsified, so every allocation and every
charge is computed centrally. The device handles map display, location smoothing, local alarms and
an offline queue.

\section{Working without a connection}

The driver application treats its own local storage as authoritative. A driver can complete an
entire journey with no signal: boarding scans, arrival marks and cash records are stored locally and
transmitted when connectivity returns, each carrying a key that prevents duplication.

Booking and payment remain online-only, because a seat cannot honestly be promised without checking
that it is free.

\section{Matching}

Assignment runs as a six-stage pipeline. A geographic filter narrows candidates, a feasibility gate
removes anything impossible or harmful, an insertion heuristic always produces a valid answer within
milliseconds, and an optimiser improves on that answer within a fixed deadline. If the optimiser is
interrupted at any moment, the earlier answer remains valid. No passenger ever waits on a solver.

Two findings shaped this. First, published measurements show that collecting requests for a short
window before matching them together substantially outperforms matching each request instantly:
reported average total waiting fell from roughly 609 seconds to roughly 427 seconds, and detour
delay from roughly 190 to roughly 75 seconds. Second, a study using Munich trip data found an
advanced matcher served up to 8\,\% more requests and saved 10\,\% more distance than a simple
heuristic, but exceeded real-time limits as the problem grew --- which is why the fast answer is
always kept.

\section{The fairness rule}

Because passengers do not declare a destination, the system cannot promise an individual arrival
time. Instead it protects the published timetable: no collection, deviation or wait may put the
vehicle more than a defined margin behind schedule. Every passenger on a route shares one schedule,
so protecting the schedule protects all of them.

\section{Configuration rather than code}

Fares, detour limits, waiting times, reward values, batch windows and service areas are stored as
data and changed from the dashboard. Sixty-five parameters are catalogued with type, permitted
range, default, who may change them, and --- importantly --- what the system does if the value is
missing or unreadable. Published guidance on runtime configuration notes that many incidents occur
during fallback rather than during the change itself, so each parameter declares its own safe
behaviour: cash defaults to disabled, refund limits default to zero, and safety categories default
to treating everything as severe.

Opening a new route, changing a price or launching a promotion requires no software release. The
same mechanism is what allows a new city to be added as configuration.

\section{Verification}

A single command runs type checking, linting, module-boundary checks, unit tests, integration tests,
contract tests, a build of every application, a secret scan and database migration checks. Nothing
is merged unless it passes. Money handling carries the strictest testing requirement, and monetary
values are stored as integers, never as floating-point numbers.

% ===================== 6. TRUST, SAFETY & GOVERNANCE =====================
\chapter{Trust, safety and governance}

\section{Verification of drivers}

Every driver is reviewed by a person before carrying anyone: national identity document, driving
licence, vehicle licence, a photograph checked against the identity document, and vehicle
photographs. Automation may pre-check image quality and expiry dates, but never grants approval.
Document expiry is monitored, with warnings at thirty, fourteen, seven and one day, and automatic
suspension on expiry.

\section{In-journey safety}

An emergency control is reachable in one action from any journey screen and sends identity, live
position, vehicle and driver details to an on-call responder. It can be triggered silently, in which
case the system deliberately makes no sound --- an audible alert could place the passenger in
greater danger. A journey can be shared as a live link with a family member, and passengers and
drivers can contact each other without either seeing the other's telephone number.

\section{How a driver is removed}

Ratings are recorded but carry no automatic consequence. Removal happens through the complaint
process, which is therefore built to carry that weight:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{48mm}Y@{}}
\toprule
\thead{Serious categories} & Immediate precautionary suspension pending investigation. The person stops driving first and is investigated second. \\
\thead{Complaint rate} & A review is triggered at a complaint rate of 10\,\% of completed journeys, subject to a minimum of twenty journeys, measured over the last hundred. \\
\thead{Repeated pattern} & Three complaints in the same category within thirty days trigger review even below that rate. \\
\thead{Human decision} & No automatic removal in any circumstance. A person decides, records a reason, and the driver may appeal. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The 10\,\% figure was checked against published practice. Major platforms deactivate on a rating
average of roughly 4.6 out of 5, which corresponds to approximately 8--12\,\% of passengers rating
poorly. The minimum-volume and rolling-window conditions exist because a percentage alone punishes
new drivers for a single complaint.

\section{Money controls}

Every movement of value is a double-entry record in one append-only ledger. Entries are never
edited; corrections are new compensating entries. Support staff can cancel a journey and issue a
refund up to one fare, three times a day, and cannot see identity documents or alter balances.
Managers set prices but cannot move money. Two-factor authentication is mandatory for all staff.
Promotional budgets are enforced in the ledger, so a campaign cannot overspend its allocation.

\section{Data}

All operational data is retained, with older records moved to lower-cost storage rather than
deleted. Movement history is held separately with its own encryption, analytical access is
pseudonymised by default, and re-identification is a separate action restricted to the highest
administrative role and recorded. Bulk export requires approval and is logged.

\begin{notebox}
\notehead{Referred to counsel}
Indefinite retention of personal movement data, and the regulatory position of the service under
Egyptian transport law, are matters for legal counsel. They are recorded in the specification as
open items and are not assessed here.
\end{notebox}

\section{Deliberate omissions}

No revenue, user or growth figures appear in this document because the service has not operated. No
market size estimate is given because the underlying measurement has not been done. No timeline or
hiring plan is included, as those decisions sit with the founder. No valuation or funding terms are
proposed here.

% ===================== 8. ROUTE TO LAUNCH =====================
\chapter{Route to launch}

The plan is sequential: one corridor working completely before widening. Each phase ends at a
demonstrable condition rather than a date.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{8mm}L{40mm}Y@{}}
\toprule
\thead{} & \thead{Phase} & \thead{Ends when} \\
\midrule
0 & Foundations & The repository, automated checks, database, configuration system and audit trail exist and are deployable. \\
1 & Identity and vehicles & A real driver can be verified and approved by a real administrator. \\
2 & Geography & The launch corridor is fully surveyed and its boarding points verified. \\
3 & The core journey & A passenger books, walks to a stop, boards by code and pays --- on one corridor. \\
4 & Safety and support & A passenger in difficulty reaches a person, and an unsafe driver can be removed. \\
5 & Commercial control & A manager can change a price and run a promotion without engineering involvement. \\
6 & Recurring travel & A commuter buys a monthly subscription and travels without further action. \\
7 & Validation and launch & Simulation sets the operating parameters; a closed trial on one corridor meets defined criteria; public launch follows. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\vspace{4pt}
Widening beyond the first corridor is a dashboard action, not an engineering project. That is a
direct consequence of holding all commercial values in configuration.

\section{Before construction begins}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
\item Survey the launch corridor's map data and confirm the routing engine performs adequately on it.
\item Obtain infrastructure quotations.
\item Obtain a regulatory opinion from Egyptian counsel.
\item Commission the visual design from the existing design system and screen definitions.
\item Write the passenger-facing text in Arabic and English.
\end{enumerate}
